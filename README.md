# Household Insurance Expenditures: An Econometric Analysis of Spending Patterns and Household Characteristics

## Overview

This project examines household insurance expenditures and the relationship between housing expenditure burden and insurance spending patterns.

Using data from the **Consumer Expenditure Survey (CES)** conducted by the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, the study investigates how housing burden relates to both the likelihood of reporting positive insurance expenditures and the amounts households spend across different insurance categories.

This paper represents a continuation and expansion of the author's earlier research, *Housing Burden and Insurance Expenditures*. Building on that initial investigation, the current study applies a broader econometric framework to examine insurance expenditure participation and spending amounts across multiple insurance categories.

The broader objective of this research is to better understand household expenditure patterns and the relationship between housing affordability and other household financial obligations.

## Research Continuity

This paper builds upon the earlier research project, *Housing Burden and Insurance Expenditures*, which examined the relationship between housing burden and household insurance expenditures.

The current study extends that work by:

- Expanding the examination of insurance expenditure categories.
- Distinguishing between the likelihood of having positive insurance expenditures and the amount spent.
- Applying bias-reduced logistic regression and Gamma generalized linear models.
- Incorporating additional household characteristics and state and quarter indicators.
- Examining differences in insurance expenditure patterns across household tenure groups.

Together, these projects represent an ongoing research effort examining household affordability, expenditure decisions, and the financial pressures associated with household living costs.

## Research Objectives

The primary objectives of this study are to:

- Examine differences in insurance expenditures across household tenure groups.
- Investigate the relationship between housing burden and the likelihood of positive insurance expenditures.
- Analyze the relationship between housing burden and insurance expenditure amounts.
- Examine how household characteristics are associated with insurance spending patterns.
- Compare results across different categories of insurance.

## Data

**Source:** U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, Consumer Expenditure Survey (CES).

The analysis uses approximately 91,991 household-quarter observations covering a 24-quarter study period.

The study examines five insurance expenditure outcomes:

1. Total insurance expenditures
2. Vehicle insurance expenditures
3. Life insurance expenditures
4. Personal insurance expenditures
5. Health insurance expenditures

The primary explanatory variable is housing burden, measured as housing expenditures relative to gross household income.

The analysis also incorporates household characteristics, state indicators, and quarter indicators.

## Methodology

The study employs several statistical and econometric techniques:

- Descriptive statistics to summarize household characteristics and insurance expenditure patterns.
- One-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) to examine expenditure differences across tenure groups.
- Tukey's honestly significant difference (HSD) tests for pairwise group comparisons.
- Bias-reduced logistic regression to examine the likelihood of positive insurance expenditures.
- Gamma generalized linear models (GLMs) with a log link to examine insurance expenditure amounts among observations with positive expenditures.

The logistic and Gamma models provide complementary perspectives by examining expenditure participation and expenditure amounts separately.

## Key Research Findings

The findings indicate that the relationship between housing burden and insurance expenditures varies across insurance categories.

The logistic regression models identify differences in the association between housing burden and the likelihood of positive insurance expenditures.

The Gamma regression models show that housing burden is positively associated with expected total, life, and health insurance expenditures, while negative associations are observed for vehicle and personal insurance expenditures.

The results highlight the importance of examining insurance expenditure participation separately from expenditure amounts.

## Limitations

This study has several limitations:

- The analysis is observational and does not establish causal relationships.
- Survey weights are not incorporated into the analysis.
- The findings should not be interpreted as nationally representative estimates.
- The analysis is limited to the available CES sample and study period.
- Household-quarter observations may include repeated observations from the same consumer unit.
